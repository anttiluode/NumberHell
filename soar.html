Soar

An eye of 16 numbers flies over a world of 4,096 cells. It sees the world as a 4×4 picture at whatever height it is flying: from the sky one number covers 256 cells, at ground level one cell. Flat view: it climbs. Something sparkles: it dives toward it. At ground level it eats. Sparkles appear near three hidden springs that wander.

  • In a world 256 times bigger than the eye, the head wins. This morning a fixed wire beat the head because brain and world were the same size. Here a fixed window catches about 1 in 250 sparkles; the flying eye catches over 80%.
  • All the skill is in the climb-and-dive rule. The eye's 16 numbers run this morning's NumberHell rule (the world enters as gain), and ignoring them changes nothing.
  • Writing into the world hurt. A lure left where food was found traps the eye: it keeps diving onto its own memories, and eats a tenth as much. A warning costs about 30%, because food really does come back near the springs. Memory in the world is only worth something if what it says stays true.

The rule

  • Look: the 4×4 view at height L averages blocks of 2^L × 2^L cells (L = 0 ground … 4 the whole world).
  • Steer: move toward the brightest of the 16 view cells. If it stands well above the rest of the view, drop one level; if the view is flat, climb one.
  • Eat: at L = 0, sparkles inside the 4×4-cell window are eaten. Optionally the world keeps a mark there that later looks like food (lure) or like a hole (warning); marks fade.

Not new: zooming in on contrast is coarse-to-fine search, and the climb-and-dive is close to how foveating vision models and quadtree searches work. It is a toy for watching an eye find things in a world far bigger than itself.
