Number Hell

One vector of 256 numbers that reads itself. Its position numbers are 8 bits, so it has 8 one-dimensional chessboards, with squares of size 1, 2, 4 … 128. Multiplying chessboards gives every one of its 256 patterns. A head walks down the zoom levels to find the pattern the vector is loudest in, and the vector is rewritten by what the head found.

At level k the vector is cut into coarse blocks of 2^k fine numbers. Each fine block is multiplied by a small chessboard and summed; the squares add up to the energy of a whole family of patterns. The head keeps the louder of two families, 2 readings per level, 16 per tick, instead of checking all 256 patterns.

All 256 patterns · energy now

Yellow: the pattern the head picked. Red: the two loudest patterns p and q, and green their product p⊕q (multiply the boards, XOR the bits). The rule multiplies the vector by a shifted copy of itself, and multiplying chessboards makes new chessboards, so loud patterns tend to come in closed families.

History · each column is one tick, each row one bit of the chosen board (square size 1 at top)

A board held for many ticks shows as flat stripes, a two-board alternation as a fine weave, wandering as noise. Which you get depends on ε, gain and who picks.

The rule

Each tick the head picks a board a (8 bits). Then every number is mixed with its product with its partner at position i XOR a, the number the board pairs it with:

x[i] ← (1−ε)·x[i] + ε·tanh( gain · x[i] · x[i XOR a] ) + noise then subtract the mean (the flat board) and rescale to RMS 1

So the vector chooses the address it reads, and what it reads changes the vector that will choose the next address: xnext = W(x)·x, where W is built from the vector itself. Nothing is learned and nothing is stored except the 256 numbers.

What was measured

Offline, in Python, with the same rule: 30 random starts per row, 400 ticks each, then the share of the vector’s energy in the closed family {p, q, p⊕q} of its two loudest patterns. A random vector puts about 0.4% of its energy in each pattern, so about 1.2% in any three.

  • Closed families come from the product rule itself. They appear with random boards too.
  • Letting the head pick roughly doubles the concentration at moderate mixing. At the strongest setting random picking concentrates more. So self-reading changes the dynamics; it is not simply better.
  • Without subtracting the mean the loop collapses onto the flat board in every run: a number times itself is never negative.

What this is not

  • Not new mathematics. The 8 chessboards are Rademacher functions and their products Walsh functions. The tree walk is the search of Goldreich–Levin (1989) and Kushilevitz–Mansour (1991), here greedy and fed back into the signal. A matrix built from the state is what attention does.
  • Not a learner and not a benchmark. It is a dynamical toy for watching a vector reorganise how it is read.
  • Not covered by the VP ≠ VNP paper. Each zoom level is a cut of the 8 bits, the kind of cut that paper prices, but choosing the board from the state is branching, and the paper is about polynomial circuits without it.
